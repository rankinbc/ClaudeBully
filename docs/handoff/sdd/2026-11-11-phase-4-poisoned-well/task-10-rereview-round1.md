## The transcript ceiling (M5)

- **Every message is kept.** `cut_to_ceiling` never drops one. Over the ceiling it finds the largest per-message cap that fits and cuts each longer message to its head and tail around `CUT_MARK`. `MIN_MESSAGE_CHARS` (200) is a floor: a ceiling too small for the message count is overrun, not met by dropping a message. Probe 6: 300 random cases keep every message, and the output is equal across two calls; probe 6c: for 195 cases that needed a cut, the binary search gives the same cap as a brute-force scan; a long message keeps its first and last characters; short messages are never cut first; 100 messages of 1000 characters under a ceiling of 1000 all stay, cut, at 23,000 characters in all (the stated overrun: 100 messages cannot fit 1000 characters).
- **The cut is marked and deterministic.** Each cut message holds `[... n characters cut ...]` (`metrics/contradictions.CUT_MARK`, imported), and the heading tells the judge the ceiling and the mark. There is no randomness and no clock.
- **The total fits.** `_transcript` reserves each label and newline, so the transcript is at most 200,000 characters (probe 6b: a 308,000-character reply gives a transcript of at most 200,000 characters and holds the mark). The prompt also holds the definition, the heading and the reveal line, which adds about 1.5 KB.
- **The cache key covers the transcript as shown.** The key is `JudgeCache.key(model, prompt)` and the prompt holds the transcript after the cut. Probe 6b: changing one character in the cut-away middle of a 308,000-character reply leaves the key unchanged (it is not shown, so it is not in the key); changing the first character moves it. A cached verdict is therefore a verdict on what the judge saw.
- **The review's recommendation is followed.** No 1200-character cut under the ceiling, so a loosening in the middle of an ordinary long reply is read in full. At the first review's sizing (34 exchanges, replies of 1 to 2 KB) nothing is cut.

## New findings

### Minor

**R1-m1. The stale rule skips the whole file, and reads every conversation of the run, rated or not.** `ratings.py:313-315` (`conversation_sizes` covers every conversation), `ratings.py:366-388`, `run_analysis.py:188-200`.
- *Scenario:* a run with two reps, both judged `true`. Rep 1 resumes and grows. The plain `analyze` that follows warns that rep 1 changed, and applies no flag at all: probe 8 shows `(rep 0 flagged, rep 1 flagged) = (False, False)`. Rep 0 did not change, and its flag was still valid. Also `conversations` holds every conversation, so any unrelated cell that resumes (a plain cell with no verdict) makes the file stale. The warning is on stderr only, and `results.md` shows the rep as a success with no note.
- *Why it matters:* a valid judge flag stops keeping its rep out of the success counts, in the direction that favors the bully. It is the staleness rule's own cost; the first review (M6) suggested applying "only the keys that still match".
- *Fix:* in `stale_reason`'s caller, apply each verdict whose `<cell>/<rep>` still has its recorded turn count and warn naming the rest; keep the whole-file skip for a `prompt_version` mismatch only. Record `conversations` only for conversations that hold a verdict. At the least, add one line to `results.md` when a file was skipped as stale.
